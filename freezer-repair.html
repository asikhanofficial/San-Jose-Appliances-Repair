Standalone freezers—whether horizontal chest units or upright vertical models—are designed to maintain temperatures consistently below 0°F (-18°C). When an internal component begins to fail, the degradation is often silent until food starts softening.

Symptom Checker

Common Freezer Problems We Repair

If your freezer shows any of these symptoms, prompt attention avoids food spoilage.

!

Freezer Warming Up (Food Thawing)

Failed compressor start capacitor, open overload protector, dirty condenser coils, or defective temperature control cold control.

!

Heavy Snow-Like Frost Buildup

Cracked or hardened magnetic door perimeter gasket allowing warm room humidity inside, or failed auto-defrost heater.

!

Compressor Runs Continuously

Defective thermostat failing to detect freezing temperatures, restricted airflow around coils, or low refrigerant charge.

!

Loud Buzzing or Rattle From Behind

Dry bearings in the condenser cooling fan motor, loose compressor mounting grommets, or vibrating copper capillary tubes.

!

Water Pooling Under the Freezer

Cracked condensation drain pan, plugged drain spout during manual or auto-defrost cycle, or leveling tilt issues.

!

Freezer Clicking Every Few Minutes

The compressor's thermal overload protector cycling off because the compressor is locked or the start PTC thermistor is burned.

What Our Freezer Repair Service Covers

When our technician evaluates your freezer in San Jose, we follow a comprehensive diagnostic procedure:

  • Compressor start relay, run capacitor, and winding resistance testing
  • Cold control thermostat testing and mechanical contact inspection
  • Auto-defrost system heater element, bimetal thermostat, and timer checks
  • Magnetic door gasket alignment and seal compression verification
  • Post-repair operational temperature verification and compressor cycling check

What to Do Before Calling

Keep the freezer lid or door tightly closed to retain existing cold air, check that the unit hasn't been accidentally unplugged, and note the model number.

Thermal Blanket Tip

If power or cooling is lost, covering the closed unit with a clean heavy blanket can help preserve sub-zero temperatures for several hours.
